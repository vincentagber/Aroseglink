The first step is to get admission to a Canadian Designated Learning Institution (DLI) recognized by the government of Canada. Once an individual receives a letter of acceptance from a DLI, he or she may be able to apply for a study permit. Applications may be made online or by mail.

Tuition fees vary based on the institution and your chosen course.

Processing times for study permits vary by visa office. Please refer to the official list of processing times based on your local visa office.

An applicant is not required to obtain a study permit for a program which is less than six months in duration. However, if a course is longer than six months, the prospective student will need to apply for a study permit.

Please note that some Canadian Universities and colleges may require IELTS or proof of language proficiency in order to gain admission depending on the course of study.

Medical Exam: An applicant may be required to undergo a medical examination based on the discretion of the visa officer.

Police Certificate: An applicant may need a criminal record check if he or she intends to come to Canada as a student. Police certificates are required to determine if applicants have a criminal record and help visa officers make sure applicants are not a security risk to Canada.

An international student may be eligible to work while studying in Canada. A student must have a valid study permit and be enrolled full-time at a Designated Learning Institution (DLI). Study permit holders may be allowed to work for up to 20 hours per week during regular academic sessions and full time during scheduled breaks.

Canada study permit applicants need to prove they have sufficient funds to cover tuition fees, and financial capacity to support living expenses for the first year of study. Typically this is Tuition plus $10,000 for a 12-month period for a single student. Additional funds are required for accompanying family members.

Canadian immigration policy offers international students many ways to stay and settle in the country after graduation. A popular option is the Post-Graduation Work Permit Program (PGWPP). Gaining work experience in Canada after graduation may help you qualify for permanent residence.

Spouse: A spouse or common-law partner of a valid study permit holder may be eligible to apply for an open work permit.

Children: Dependent children may accompany you to Canada. A Temporary Resident Visa (TRV) or ETA may be required. Minor children may be eligible to study without a study permit if accompanying parents are already in Canada on a study or work permit.

Processing fees and administrative charges vary depending on the destination country, chosen university or college, program level, and specific visa category.

Please contact our admissions advisory team or speak with a counselor for a detailed assessment and fee schedule tailored to your chosen study destination.

There are three main requirements for those who want to study in the UK:
  • You have been offered a place on a course and received a CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance of Studies)
  • You can speak, read, write and understand English
  • You have enough money to support yourself and pay for your course

  1. Apply for your programme of choice and receive an offer.
  2. Send requested documents to be checked by the Admissions team.
  3. Pay a deposit and CAS payment.
  4. Your CAS will be issued, allowing you to apply for your visa up to 6 months before your course begins.
  5. You may be invited to a credibility interview.
  6. You will be contacted by UKVI regarding the outcome.

You may be required to attend a credibility interview via video link. The purpose is for an Entry Clearance Officer to establish that you are a genuine student. You should prepare to answer honestly why you chose your specific university and course, why you want to study in the UK, and details about your plans. You must not look like you are repeating pre-prepared answers.
